Excise Duty

Duty charged on particular goods, collected from the customer for the state.

VAT New duty
Excise is not VAT and does not sit beside it
Excise forms part of the value VAT is then charged on, so on an invoice line the duty is added to the net first and VAT is worked out on the sum. Charging both on the net would understate VAT; charging excise on the VAT-inclusive figure would overstate the duty. The duty is credited to a liability and never to income — the company collects it from the customer on the state's behalf and it was never the company's to earn, so revenue is unaffected. A duty is attached to products, because excise is charged on particular goods rather than on trade in general.
An unconfirmed duty is never charged
A duty can be created and edited freely, but it reaches no invoice until somebody has read its rate against the current schedule and confirmed it. Excise rates and the list of goods they cover change with each finance law, so a rate nobody has checked is worse than none. Changing what a duty charges withdraws its confirmation; renaming it does not.
